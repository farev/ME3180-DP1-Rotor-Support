ME 3180 · Design Problem 2 · Rotor shaft

Every force on the rotor shaft

Loads and dimensions from the DP2 assignment (Rev 2, Figure 2). First-pass statics with the shaft diameter not yet chosen. Due 13 November 2026, 11:59 pm.

Start here

The rotor shaft is a vertical steel rod held by two bearings 300 mm apart. Below the lower bearing it carries the sprocket, then the rotor hub, and the river pushes on the rotor 500 mm down.

That 560 N push, about the weight of a 57 kg person, acts on a long lever. The lower bearing has to push back with about 1.5 kN, and the upper bearing pulls the other way with about 0.94 kN to stop the shaft tipping. Compared with that, the 19.6 N·m of torque is small, and it only travels the 120 mm of shaft between the pin in the hub and the key in the sprocket.

The shaft spins at 285 rpm while the river always pushes the same way, so every point on its surface is stretched and squeezed once per turn. That is fatigue loading. The worst place is the retaining-ring groove just under the lower bearing, where the bending is largest and the groove concentrates stress the most.

Section 2Applied loads

Everything that acts on the rotating assembly from outside, before the supports react. The weights act along the shaft axis, so they produce no bending; only the drag and the chain bend the shaft.

Chain force components

A rotation by θ about +y takes x̂ to (cos θ, 0, −sin θ). With θ = −4.732°, the chain force points slightly toward +z.

Section 3Bearing reactions (rubric item 1)

The bearings are short, so each is a simple support: radial force only, no moment. With the lower bearing L at yL and the upper bearing U at yU, each horizontal direction is a beam on two supports with an overhang. Take moments about L, then sum forces.

Section 4Internal loads along the shaft

Cut the shaft at height y and keep the part below the cut. V is the sum of the horizontal forces below the cut, M is their moment about the cut, T is the torque that has entered below the cut and not yet left, and N is the axial tension from the weight hanging below. The diagrams run vertically, the same way as the shaft.

Section 5Where the stress peaks (rubric items 2 and 3)

The shaft has one constant diameter, so the stress raisers are only the features machined into it: two ring grooves, the keyway and the cross hole for the pin. The table combines the internal loads at each feature with Shigley's first-iteration stress-concentration factors. Click a row to put the cut there.

Reading the table

The bending stress is fully reversed (σm ≈ 0, σa = Kf·32M/πd³) because the loads stay fixed while the shaft turns. The torque is steady (τa = 0, τm = Kfs·16T/πd³). The weight adds a tiny steady tension. These numbers rank the sections; the shaft design itself uses Kf = 1 + q(Kt − 1) with the real notch radii, the Marin factors and DE-Goodman (Shigley Eq. 7-8).

Section 6Equilibrium check

All applied loads and reactions summed about the origin. Every residual should be zero to rounding.
