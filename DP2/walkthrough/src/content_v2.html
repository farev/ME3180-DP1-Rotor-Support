RReview verdict

Rev P3 passes every Rev2 requirement with the Piazza-approved shaft properties. The geometry is the P2 design; only the shaft stock changed, after the approved 1045 yield strength (75 ksi) came in below the 598 MPa we had proposed and the cost rule was read as the full price of the shortest sufficient stock. Items marked ⚠ are corrections or improvements for the report.

Verified

Arithmetic. Reactions, moments, Marin factors, notch sensitivity, all five section checks, the key, screw, pin and ring checks and the BOM total were rebuilt from scratch with vector statics (r × F about each cut). Every value matches the provisional PDF to four decimals.

Verified

Groove Kt = 3.5 is conservative. Reading Shigley Fig. A-15-16 (11th ed., p. 1047) at a/t = 1.51: the r/t = 0.40 curve gives 3.40 and r/t = 0.60 gives 3.17, so r/t = 0.478 gives Kt ≈ 3.3. Using 3.5 adds about 6 %.

Verified

Shaft choice (Rev P3). A solver (DP2/solver) ran every McMaster rotary shaft and every stainless ring for its diameter through these same equations. 1346K534 (1566, HRC25, 1-1/2 × 24 in, $112.69) is the cheapest that passes fatigue and both yield checks. 4138N27 (1-11/16 in 1045) is $20 cheaper but fails the added Kt check. See Step 10.

⚠ Method

Yield check uses Kt, Shigley uses Kf. Shigley Eq. (7-15) builds σ′max with Kf and Kfs. With Kf = 2.72 at the groove, ny = 3.96 instead of 3.08. Report both. The keyseat governs the Shigley check at 3.63 (needs Sy ≥ 501 MPa); the strict groove check needs Sy ≥ 591 MPa. The approved 606.7 MPa clears both.

Fixed

Hardness conversion. Shigley 11th ed. Eq. (2-36) is Sut = 3.4 HB MPa. For the 1566 shaft, HRC25 = HB 253, so Sut = 3.4(253) = 860.2 MPa. Piazza lists only Sy, so this hardness minimum is used.

⚠ Drawing

Groove radius tolerance is not machinable. R 0.0098–0.0100 in is a 5 µm band. R 0.008–0.010 in (0.20–0.25 mm) is inspectable and within Smalley's 0.010 in limit. At rmin = 0.20 mm the chart gives Kt ≈ 3.45 ≤ 3.5, so the result is unchanged.

Changed P2

Key lengthened to 1/2 in. The class rule says a key must be longer than it is wide, so the 1/4 in key is out. The 1/2 in key gives n = 27.5 in shear and 23.5 in bearing and halves the key pressure on the keyseat (17.5 MPa). The slot is 22.45 mm.

⚠ Practice

Set-screw torque 0.75 ± 0.02 N·m. That is about 1/13 of Shigley Table 7-4's seating torque for a 1/4 in screw (87 lbf·in = 9.8 N·m). It is chosen so the 18-8 screw stays under 205 MPa. The ±0.02 N·m band is tighter than a typical torque driver holds (about ±6 %). The checks already use the 0.73–0.77 N·m range, so the numbers stand; consider stating the tool accuracy.

Verified

Notch sensitivity. q = 0.688 (Shigley Eqs. 6-33, 6-35, checked in US and SI forms). The design still passes with q up to 0.91. With Kf = Kt everywhere (as the reference design does), only a 1-15/16 in shaft passes, at $306.55.

Resolved

Shaft properties. Sy = 88 ksi for 1566 at HRC25, D ≤ 1.5 in, is on the instructor's approved list (10 Nov). No request is needed.

Chain force components

The chain pulls with 161.8 N at θ = −4.732° from x, measured about y. A rotation θ about +y takes x̂ to (cos θ, 0, −sin θ):

\[F_{c,x}=F_c\cos\theta=161.8\cos(-4.732^\circ)=161.25\ \text{N}\]
(1)
\[F_{c,z}=-F_c\sin\theta=-161.8\sin(-4.732^\circ)=+13.35\ \text{N}\]
(2)

The +z component adds to the drag, which is the conservative sign. The weights act along the axis: rotor 120 N (through the pin), sprocket 35 N (through key and set screw) and shaft self-weight:

\[W_{sh}=\rho g\frac{\pi D^2}{4}L=7850(9.807)\frac{\pi(0.0381)^2}{4}(0.554)=48.62\ \text{N}\]
(3)
\[N=W_r+W_s+W_{sh}=120+35+48.62=203.62\ \text{N}\]
(4)

Using the full Ø38.1 cylinder slightly overestimates the weight of the machined shaft.

Hub model check

A statically consistent hub contact (−1820 N at y = −160, +2380 N at y = −240) sums to 560 N with moment −280 N·m about y = 0. The pin-hole check instead uses the larger cantilever value at the hole edge (181 N·m). Average contact pressure in a 10 mm band: 2380/(38.05 × 10) = 6.26 MPa, covered by a 12 MPa mean-stress allowance at the hole.

05Where the highest stress is (Shaft item 2)

The shaft has one diameter and no shoulders, so the only stress raisers are the two ring grooves, the keyseat and the pin hole. The largest nominal moment is at the lower bearing midplane (284.5 N·m), but there is no notch there. Half a bearing width lower, the lower retaining-ring groove combines almost the same moment (280.5 N·m) with the sharpest notch (Kt 3.5) and the smallest section (root Ø37.06 mm). It carries no torque.

The keyseat upper end comes second: slightly less moment (266.5 N·m), a milder notch, and the full 19.63 N·m torque. The pin hole sees much less moment (181 N·m). The upper groove sees no bending or torque.

Result (Shaft items 2–3)

Highest stress: lower ring groove, M = 280.5 N·m, T = 0, with N = 203.6 N axial tension. Highest fatigue demand in the conservative model: keyseat upper end, M = 266.5 N·m, T = 19.63 N·m. Step 9 confirms the ranking: the groove governs yield, the keyseat governs fatigue.

07Ring groove geometry and Kt

\[d_{min}=1.459(25.4)=37.0586\ \text{mm},\qquad t_{max}=\frac{38.1000-37.0586}{2}=0.5207\ \text{mm}\]

Decision D5

Use Kt = 3.5, rounded up from the chart. The nominal stress is referred to the groove root d, as the chart defines σ0 = 4F/(πd²) + 32M/(πd³).

Review note

Specify the radius as R 0.008–0.010 in (0.20–0.25 mm) instead of a 0.0002 in band. At r = 0.20 mm, r/t = 0.39 and the chart gives Kt ≈ 3.45, still under 3.5.
