ME 3180 · Design Problem 1 · PicoPower rotor support

A rotor support that moves 0.627 mm

Final design: a 6061-T6 6×4×3/16 in tube, two 304 stainless ¼×5 in diagonals at a = 660 mm and three ¾ in quick-release pins. Rotor-CG deflection 0.627 mm against a 0.70 mm limit, lowest factor of safety 2.31, bill of materials $598.65.
16 October 2026 · Georgia Tech ME 3180 · solver in DP1_Solver/, McMaster catalog read 8 to 14 October 2026.

Start here — no maths required

A small water turbine hangs under a metal arm, the way a lantern hangs from a bracket on a wall. The river pushes the turbine sideways with 560 newtons, about the weight of a 57 kg person.

The arm has to hold it nearly still: the turbine's centre of mass may move at most 0.7 mm, about the thickness of a credit card. This design keeps it to 0.627 mm with an aluminium tube, two stainless steel straps braced up to the wall, and three pins you can pull out by hand. The parts cost $598.65.

Drag the model to look around it. Forces shows every push and pull on the arm. Deflection exaggerates the bending a few hundred times so you can see where the 0.627 mm comes from; most of it is the arm twisting, because the river pushes on the turbine half a metre below the arm. Then change the parts above the model, or where the straps attach, and watch the design pass or fail and the price change.

What this page shows

  1. Every force on the beam. Nine applied loads and four reactions from report Table 1, solved from six equilibrium equations; they balance to 2×10−12 N.
  2. Where the 0.627 mm comes from. Beam twist (0.325 mm), sideways bending (0.186 mm) and unequal stretching of the two straps (0.143 mm) make up almost all of it, all in the direction of the river's push.
  3. The working behind each number, with the formula, the substitution and the textbook reference. It follows whatever design is in the figure.
  4. Why this design. Pull a below 600 mm and the deflection fails; push it toward 800 mm and the compressed strap starts to buckle and two strap lengths no longer fit in one 6 ft bar. The 6061 strap from the report draft passes at 0.687 mm, too close to the limit.

Everything below is the technical version. Section 4 has the deflection working; the Solo buttons in the figure isolate each effect.

Section 1The structure and its coordinates

Origin at pin O on the fixed frame. x runs along the beam toward the rotor end at x = −1300 mm (the beam overhangs 50 mm past O, so it is 1350 mm long); y is up; z points downstream, the direction of the drag. Pin A, where the diagonals meet the beam, is at x = −a; pin B is 500 mm above O. The rotor shaft is at x = −1200 mm with its CG 350 mm below the beam's bottom face, and the generator sits on the beam at x = −197.0 mm.

Section 2Forces on the beam

The free body is the beam with everything mounted on it, cut free at pin O, at pin A and at the chain. Six unknowns (Of,x, Or,x, Oy, Oz, Pf, Pr) follow from ΣF = 0 and ΣMO = 0, with Oy split equally between the two brackets (Requirement 10). Forces in N, positions in mm.

Why the diagonals carry the twist

Section 3Internal loads along the beam

Section cuts along x give the loads each deflection integrates. Torque between the rotor box and A twists the tube, My bends it sideways, Mz bends it vertically and N squeezes it between A and the generator.

Section 4Deflection of the rotor CG

The beam is analysed in a frame clamped at pin O. The diagonals then set a small rigid rotation about O so that the pin-A end stays on the (slightly stretched) plates. Because the CG hangs |yCG| = h/2 + 350 mm below the beam axis, any roll θx moves it sideways by θx·yCG. Requirement 12 is checked as elastic deflection with ideal pins. Units: N, mm, MPa.

Section 5Strength checks

Every factor-of-safety check, weakest first, against the yield strength McMaster lists for each part (or an annealed minimum where it lists only hardness). Required n = 2. The pin, bearing, net-section and Euler checks use the report's formulas; the rest come from the solver. The solver's beam check combines peak normal and peak shear stress, so it is more conservative than the report's corner-point check (n = 40 for the final design).
